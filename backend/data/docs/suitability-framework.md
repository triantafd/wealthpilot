# Suitability Assessment Framework

**WealthPilot Advisers Ltd** — fictional firm, synthetic document. The regulator
referred to below, the Office of Investment Conduct, is also fictional.

## 1. The three dimensions

Suitability is assessed on three separate dimensions. They are related but must
never be collapsed into one score.

| Dimension | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Risk tolerance | How much volatility is the client *willing* to accept? |
| Capacity for loss | How much loss can the client *absorb* without material harm? |
| Investment horizon | How long until the money is needed? |

A client may be willing to take substantial risk and still have a low capacity
for loss. Where the two disagree, **capacity for loss governs**. An adviser may
never recommend a portfolio whose risk exceeds the client's capacity for loss,
however willing the client is.

## 2. Risk tolerance categories

The risk questionnaire produces a score from 1 to 100. The score maps to exactly
one of four categories.

| Score | Category | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 – 25 | `conservative` | Prioritises capital stability. Accepts low returns to avoid loss. |
| 26 – 50 | `balanced` | Accepts moderate fluctuation in exchange for moderate growth. |
| 51 – 75 | `growth` | Accepts significant fluctuation for higher long-term growth. |
| 76 – 100 | `aggressive` | Accepts large fluctuation, including substantial loss, for maximum growth. |

The score is recorded alongside the category. An adviser who believes the
category is wrong must re-run the questionnaire; overriding the category while
keeping the score is not permitted, because the two would then contradict each
other in the client record.

## 3. Capacity for loss

Capacity for loss is assessed from the client's income, essential expenditure,
emergency reserves and dependants — not from their attitude to risk.

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| `low` | A loss of 10% or more would require a change to the client's standard of living. |
| `medium` | A loss of up to 25% could be absorbed without changing essential expenditure. |
| `high` | A loss of 25% or more could be absorbed with no material effect. |

A client with `low` capacity for loss may not hold a `growth` or `aggressive`
portfolio, irrespective of their risk tolerance score.

## 4. Investment horizon

The horizon is recorded in whole years and is the period until the client
expects to draw materially on the portfolio.

| Horizon | Maximum permitted category |
|---|---|
| Under 3 years | `conservative` |
| 3 to 5 years | `balanced` |
| 6 to 10 years | `growth` |
| Over 10 years | `aggressive` |

A client with a two-year horizon must be treated as conservative even with an
aggressive questionnaire score.

## 5. Investment objective

One objective is recorded per client. The objective must be coherent with the
risk tolerance category.

| Objective | Permitted categories |
|---|---|
| `preservation` | conservative |
| `income` | conservative, balanced |
| `balanced` | balanced, growth |
| `growth` | growth, aggressive |

A record pairing `aggressive` tolerance with a `preservation` objective is a
data error, not a client preference, and must be corrected before any
recommendation is made.

## 6. Review cycle

Every suitability assessment is valid for **12 months**. The next review date is
recorded as 365 days after the assessment date.

A review falling due is not in itself a breach. A review **more than 60 days
overdue** is a reportable breach under *Complaints and Breach Reporting*.

### Events requiring an early review

A review must be brought forward, regardless of the scheduled date, when any of
these occurs:

1. A change in the client's employment or income.
2. Retirement, or a change to the intended retirement date.
3. A change in dependants, including birth, death or divorce.
4. A single withdrawal exceeding 20% of portfolio value.
5. A client instruction that conflicts with their recorded profile.
6. A change to the client's investment restrictions.

## 7. Evidence

Each assessment records: the completed questionnaire and resulting score, the
capacity for loss assessment with supporting figures, the horizon and its basis,
the objective, the resulting category, and the adviser who signed it off.

An assessment missing any of these is incomplete and cannot support a
recommendation. Portfolios may continue to be held, but no new purchase may be
made until the gap is closed.

## 8. Clients with no valid assessment

Where no valid assessment exists, the firm:

- may continue to hold existing positions;
- may sell to raise cash at the client's instruction;
- may **not** purchase any new instrument;
- may **not** rebalance, even where the Investment Policy Statement would
  otherwise require it.

This is the one circumstance in which a rebalancing trigger is deliberately not
acted upon. The breach is recorded and escalated to Compliance instead.
