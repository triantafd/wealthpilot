# Complaints Handling and Breach Reporting

**WealthPilot Advisers Ltd** — fictional firm, synthetic document. The Office of
Investment Conduct (OIC) and the Investment Ombudsman Service referred to below
are fictional bodies.

## 1. What counts as a complaint

Any expression of dissatisfaction from a client, whether justified or not,
whether verbal or written, about the firm's service or an investment outcome.

A client asking why a fund fell in value is not a complaint. A client saying
they should never have been put in that fund is.

Where there is doubt, treat it as a complaint. Recording something as a
complaint that turns out not to be has no cost; the reverse does.

## 2. Complaint timescales

| Step | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge in writing | 5 business days from receipt |
| Substantive response, or explanation of delay | 4 weeks |
| Final response | 8 weeks |
| Client may refer to the Investment Ombudsman Service | After 8 weeks, or on receipt of the final response |

The 8-week clock starts on the day the complaint is received, not the day it is
recognised as one. A complaint identified late has already consumed part of its
window.

## 3. Who handles a complaint

Not the adviser who is the subject of it. Complaints are assigned to a
complaints handler independent of the relationship. The adviser must supply
records promptly but takes no part in the decision.

| Complaint value | Decision maker |
|---|---|
| Redress under £5,000 | Complaints handler |
| Redress £5,000 – £50,000 | Head of Compliance |
| Redress above £50,000 | Board, with external legal input |

## 4. Redress

Where a complaint is upheld, the client is restored to the position they would
have been in had the error not occurred. This is not the same as refunding
fees.

Redress is calculated as the difference between the actual portfolio value and
the value it would have had under the correct recommendation, plus interest at
4% per year on any cash shortfall, plus any fees charged on the incorrect
holding.

## 5. Reportable breaches

A breach is an internal failure, whether or not a client noticed or complained.
These are reportable to Compliance within **one business day** of discovery:

| Breach | Why |
|---|---|
| A trade placed in breach of a client restriction | No override exists; the control failed |
| A rebalance on an `execution_only` account | Prohibited absolutely |
| A purchase for a client with no valid suitability assessment | Prohibited absolutely |
| A trade above a threshold without the required approval | Authority control failed |
| A suitability review more than 60 days overdue | Framework section 6 |
| A trade on a `closed` or `suspended` account | Prohibited absolutely |
| A concentration limit breach not corrected within 10 business days | Policy section 6 |
| Client data disclosed to the wrong client | Data protection |

## 6. Breach procedure

1. **Stop** the activity. Do not attempt to trade your way out of a breach.
2. **Report** to Compliance within one business day of discovery.
3. **Record** what happened, when, which client and account, and the value at
   risk.
4. **Assess** client detriment. Where any exists, treat it as a complaint even
   if the client has not complained.
5. **Remediate** with Compliance approval. Remediation trades require Head of
   Investments approval regardless of value.
6. **Review** the control that failed, and record what changed.

Step 1 is the one most often got wrong. An adviser discovering a purchase in an
excluded sector must not quietly sell it; selling crystallises a loss or gain the
client did not instruct and converts a control breach into a second one.

## 7. Escalation to the regulator

The firm notifies the OIC within **10 business days** where a breach:

- affects more than 10 clients;
- involves client detriment above £100,000 in aggregate;
- indicates a systemic control failure rather than a single error;
- involves suspected fraud or deliberate concealment.

The decision to notify rests with the Head of Compliance and is not the
adviser's to make, nor to delay.

## 8. Records

| Record | Retention |
|---|---|
| Complaint file, including rejected complaints | 6 years from final response |
| Breach register entry | 6 years from closure |
| Trade proposals, approved, rejected and lapsed | 6 years |
| Suitability assessments | 6 years after the relationship ends |
| Client restrictions, including removed ones | 6 years after the relationship ends |

Removed restrictions are retained deliberately. A complaint years later may turn
on what the client had instructed at the time, and a record showing only the
current state cannot answer it.

## 9. Quarterly reporting

Compliance reports to the Board each quarter on: complaints received, upheld and
rejected; redress paid; breaches by category; overdue suitability reviews; and
unresolved concentration breaches.

Three consecutive quarters of rising breaches in one category triggers a control
review whether or not any individual breach was material.

## 10. Root cause

Every closed breach records a root cause from this list:

| Cause | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Control absent | No check existed |
| Control not followed | A check existed and was skipped |
| Control failed | A check ran and gave the wrong answer |
| Data wrong | The check ran correctly on incorrect client data |
| Judgement | The adviser made a defensible decision that proved wrong |

"Data wrong" is the most common cause and the most often mislabelled as
judgement. A recommendation made correctly against an out-of-date risk profile
is a data failure, not an adviser failure, and the remedy is to the data.
